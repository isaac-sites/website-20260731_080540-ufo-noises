## Introduction

An unresolved unidentified anomalous phenomenon (UAP) case does not, by itself, demonstrate the existence of unknown or extraordinary technology. In official investigations, "unresolved" is a technical classification meaning that available evidence is insufficient to reach a confident identification. It is not a positive finding that an object displayed exotic propulsion, non-human origins, or physics beyond current understanding. This distinction is especially important when assessing reports that include unusual or absent sounds. Without reliable telemetry, calibrated sensor data and corroborating observations, investigators cannot determine whether an unusual acoustic report reflects an unconventional object, an ordinary phenomenon seen under unusual conditions, or simply a lack of usable evidence. NASA, the U.S. Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) and the Department of Defense's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) have all stressed that uncertainty should not be confused with proof of extraordinary technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP - NASA ScienceFebruary 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 23, 2026</span></span></span>

## How official reports define an unresolved event

Official UAP programmes use "unresolved" as an evidential category rather than a judgement about what an object is. A case may remain unresolved because investigators cannot collect enough reliable information to identify it with confidence. The classification therefore reflects the limits of the available data, not confirmation of an extraordinary explanation.

Several factors commonly prevent resolution:

## Why missing telemetry prevents firm conclusions

The absence of key measurements is one of the strongest reasons investigators avoid drawing dramatic conclusions from unresolved cases.

For example, a witness may report that an object appeared silent despite moving rapidly. Without accurate information about the object's distance, trajectory and atmospheric conditions, investigators cannot determine whether:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the object was actually close enough for its sound to be heard;
* wind or temperature layers redirected the sound;
* the object was moving in a way that reduced audible noise;
* the perceived motion resulted from misjudging distance or perspective.

</div>

The same problem applies to apparent speed and manoeuvrability. If range is unknown, apparent acceleration can be greatly overestimated. Modern investigations therefore rely on multiple independent sensors because they allow estimates of position, motion and timing that eyewitness reports alone cannot provide.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP - NASA ScienceFebruary 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 23, 2026</span></span></span>

Within the broader question of [UFO noises]({{ 'ufo-noises/' | relative_url }}), this means that an unusual or absent sound cannot be interpreted in isolation. Acoustic observations become meaningful only when they are supported by independently verified measurements.

## Why uncertainty differs from evidence of exotic technology

A common misunderstanding is to treat unresolved cases as if they represent positive evidence for advanced propulsion or non-human craft. Logically and scientifically, these are different claims.

An unresolved investigation establishes only that investigators could not reach a confident identification from the available evidence. It does not establish that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* conventional explanations have been disproved;
* known physics has been violated;
* an object demonstrated impossible performance;
* an unknown technology has been confirmed.

</div>

This distinction reflects a basic principle of scientific reasoning: failure to explain an observation is not evidence for any particular alternative explanation. Multiple competing explanations may remain plausible simultaneously until additional evidence narrows the possibilities.

Official UAP reports consistently apply this principle by separating confidence in the evidence from speculation about origins.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP - NASA ScienceFebruary 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 23, 2026</span></span></span>

## What recent official investigations show

Recent U.S. government reporting illustrates how unresolved cases are handled in practice.

AARO has received hundreds of reports while resolving many others as [balloons]({{ 'balloons/' | relative_url }}), birds, aircraft, satellites or unmanned aerial systems once additional evidence became available. At the same time, numerous reports remain unresolved because they lack sufficient information for analysis. AARO has repeatedly stated that unresolved status should not be interpreted as evidence of extraterrestrial beings, activity or technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/aaro-fy24-consolidated-report/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[newparadigminstitute.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New Paradigm Instituteaaro fy24 consolidated report – New Paradigm Institute</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">November 14, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 14, 2024</span></span></span>

Similarly, AARO's historical review of decades of U.S. and international investigations concluded that although many cases remained unexplained, no official investigation reviewed found evidence that unresolved reports demonstrated extraterrestrial origin. The report specifically identified persistent problems such as missing measurements of speed, altitude and size as major barriers to reaching firm conclusions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf?emci=622cd777-b774-f011-8dc9-6045bda9d96b&amp;emdi=ea000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000001&amp;sourceid=1070813" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical Report...</span></span></span>

This means an unresolved designation is not necessarily permanent. It represents the current state of knowledge rather than an intrinsic property of the event. As investigative methods improve and additional information emerges, some previously unexplained reports move into identifiable categories.

For this reason, investigators generally avoid assigning extraordinary significance to unresolved cases until stronger evidence becomes available.

## What this means for reports of unusual sounds

Within official UAP investigations, reported noises—including humming, buzzing, roaring or complete silence—are treated as contextual observations rather than proof of unusual propulsion. Human hearing is affected by distance, terrain, atmospheric conditions and [perception]({{ 'perception/' | relative_url }}), while most historical cases lack calibrated acoustic recordings.

When a case remains unresolved, the uncertainty usually reflects missing or conflicting evidence across multiple sources, not confirmation that the reported sound originated from an unknown technology. In practice, investigators require repeatable, measurable and independently corroborated data before concluding that an observation represents something genuinely outside known explanations.

Accordingly, unresolved UAP cases should be understood as evidence gaps rather than evidence of extraordinary technology. They highlight where information is incomplete, not where unknown propulsion has been demonstrated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP - NASA ScienceFebruary 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 23, 2026</span></span></span>
