## Introduction

The Hum is one of the most persistent acoustic mysteries linked to UFO folklore. It refers to a [low-frequency]({{ 'low-frequency/' | relative_url }}) humming, buzzing or rumbling sound reported by small numbers of people in specific communities, often without an obvious source. Because the sound can be difficult to locate, is sometimes heard only by a minority of residents, and has occasionally resisted straightforward investigation, it has inspired speculation about extraterrestrial craft, secret military technology and other extraordinary explanations.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/ufo_noises_37edd1_the_hum_ufo_speculat_3d3c0a-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for The Hum" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Despite these claims, there is no credible evidence connecting The Hum to UFOs or unidentified aerial phenomena. Instead, investigations have repeatedly found that different cases appear to have different causes. Some have been traced to industrial equipment or infrastructure, while others may reflect internal auditory conditions such as low-frequency tinnitus. Rather than pointing to a single global mystery, current evidence suggests that "The Hum" is a label applied to several distinct phenomena that can produce remarkably similar experiences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.international.gc.ca/department-ministere/windsor_hum_results-bourdonnement_windsor_resultats.aspx?lang=eng&amp;pedisable=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: international.gc.ca">[Global Affairs Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">international.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Summary of the ‘Windsor Hum Study’ Results...</span></span></span>

### Kokomo, Indiana

In Kokomo, Indiana, complaints about a persistent hum emerged in the late 1990s. Unlike Taos, investigators eventually identified significant industrial contributors, including cooling towers and air compressors operating at low frequencies. After modifications were made, many residents reported improvements.

The [Kokomo case]({{ 'kokomo-case/' | relative_url }}) illustrates an important lesson: a mysterious sound can initially appear inexplicable but later prove to have an ordinary industrial origin once sufficiently detailed acoustic measurements are performed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e303828cdee01e41ed8ab4e2f2e165379d50a034&amp;repid=rep1&amp;type=pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citeseerx.ist.psu.edu">[CiteSeerX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citeseerx.ist.psu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientiﬁc Exploration, Vol. 18, No. 4, pp. 571–595, 2004November 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1, 2025</span></span></span>

### Windsor, Ontario

Beginning in 2011, thousands of residents around Windsor, Canada, reported an intermittent low-frequency vibration that was often described as being felt as much as heard. Multiple scientific studies detected genuine low-frequency acoustic activity and repeatedly pointed towards the heavily industrialised Zug Island area across the Detroit River as the most likely source.

Researchers characterised the Hum's acoustic signature and localised it geographically, although obtaining direct access to all suspected industrial facilities proved difficult. This case demonstrates that some "Hum" reports involve measurable external sound rather than purely subjective [perception]({{ 'perception/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.international.gc.ca/department-ministere/windsor_hum_results-bourdonnement_windsor_resultats.aspx?lang=eng&amp;pedisable=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: international.gc.ca">[Global Affairs Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">international.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Summary of the ‘Windsor Hum Study’ Results...</span></span></span>

## External and internal sound explanations

One reason The Hum remains controversial is that there is probably no universal explanation. Different cases appear to arise through different mechanisms.

### External environmental sources

Many investigations have identified plausible physical causes capable of producing persistent low-frequency noise:

* industrial machinery;
* large ventilation systems;
* compressors and cooling equipment;
* distant road or rail traffic;
* electrical infrastructure;
* atmospheric conditions that carry low-frequency sound over long distances.

Low-frequency sound behaves differently from higher-pitched noise. It travels farther, penetrates buildings more easily and is difficult for people to locate accurately. This makes ordinary infrastructure seem strangely directionless or omnipresent, encouraging the impression that the sound has no obvious origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.international.gc.ca/department-ministere/windsor_hum_results-bourdonnement_windsor_resultats.aspx?lang=eng&amp;pedisable=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: international.gc.ca">[Global Affairs Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">international.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Summary of the ‘Windsor Hum Study’ Results...</span></span></span>

### Internal auditory explanations

Not every reported Hum corresponds to an external sound. Increasing evidence suggests that many people experience an internally generated perception.

Research published in the *International Tinnitus Journal* found that numerous Hum hearers displayed characteristics more consistent with an unusual form of tinnitus than with exposure to an unidentified environmental signal. Features such as changes when turning the head or interactions with other sounds suggested that the perceived hum often originated within the auditory system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27488996/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manifestations of a low-frequency sound of unknown origin perceived worldwide, also known as &quot;the Hum&quot; or the &quot;Taos Hum&quot; - PubMed...</span></span></span>

More recent research has strengthened this interpretation. A 2026 study concluded that, for many participants, the most likely explanation was low-frequency subjective tinnitus rather than exceptional hearing sensitivity or an unidentified external acoustic source. Importantly, the researchers did not argue that every reported Hum has the same cause; instead, they concluded that multiple mechanisms probably exist.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hearingreview.com/inside-hearing/research/study-suggests-the-hum-is-most-likely-a-form-of-tinnitus" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hearingreview.com">[The Hearing Review]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hearingreview.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Hearing ReviewStudy Suggests &quot;The Hum&quot; Is Most Likely a Form of Tinnitus &#124; The Hearing ReviewJune 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 23, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why UFO theories persist

Although scientific investigations have not found evidence connecting The Hum with UFOs, the phenomenon continues to feature prominently in UFO discussions. Several factors help explain why.

### The sound seems unnatural

Many hearers describe the Hum as mechanical, rhythmic or electrical rather than resembling familiar environmental noise. Because it often lacks an obvious direction, listeners may conclude that it comes from an unusual airborne or underground source.

### Not everyone hears it

One of the most puzzling characteristics is that family members or neighbours standing together may disagree about whether any sound exists. For those who hear it, this can reinforce the belief that the source possesses unusual properties beyond ordinary acoustics.

In reality, hearing sensitivity varies considerably between individuals, particularly at very low frequencies, and internal auditory phenomena affect only some people.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27488996/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manifestations of a low-frequency sound of unknown origin perceived worldwide, also known as &quot;the Hum&quot; or the &quot;Taos Hum&quot; - PubMed...</span></span></span>

### Mystery attracts extraordinary explanations

Whenever investigations fail to identify a single cause, alternative theories naturally emerge. Over the years, suggestions have included:

* hidden military technology;
* underground facilities;
* electromagnetic transmissions;
* secret industrial projects;
* extraterrestrial spacecraft.

Proposed UFO connections generally rely on coincidence, personal testimony or the absence of an accepted explanation rather than positive evidence linking unidentified aerial phenomena to the reported sounds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e303828cdee01e41ed8ab4e2f2e165379d50a034&amp;repid=rep1&amp;type=pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citeseerx.ist.psu.edu">[CiteSeerX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citeseerx.ist.psu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientiﬁc Exploration, Vol. 18, No. 4, pp. 571–595, 2004November 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1, 2025</span></span></span>

## What the evidence says about a UFO connection

From an evidence-based perspective, The Hum does not support the existence of UFOs.

Several observations weigh against a direct connection:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Hum reports often continue for months or years in the same location, unlike typical UFO sightings.
* Many cases occur without any accompanying visual observations.
* Where detailed acoustic investigations have succeeded, they frequently identify industrial or environmental contributors.
* Growing medical evidence indicates that at least some reports originate within the auditory system rather than in the external environment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.international.gc.ca/department-ministere/windsor_hum_results-bourdonnement_windsor_resultats.aspx?lang=eng&amp;pedisable=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: international.gc.ca">[gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">international.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Global Affairs Canada</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Summary of the ‘Windsor Hum Study’ Results...</span></span></span>

</div>

That does not mean every reported Hum has been fully explained. Some communities continue to experience persistent low-frequency sounds whose precise sources remain uncertain. However, "unexplained" is not equivalent to "evidence of UFOs". Scientific investigations generally treat these cases as complex problems in acoustics, environmental monitoring and human perception rather than indicators of extraterrestrial technology.

## Why The Hum remains scientifically interesting

The enduring fascination of The Hum lies not in its connection to UFOs but in the way it highlights the limits of environmental sensing and human perception. It demonstrates that multiple mechanisms—including industrial noise, [atmospheric sound]({{ 'sound-travel/' | relative_url }}) propagation and internal auditory processes—can produce experiences that feel equally real to those affected.

As new acoustic monitoring techniques and hearing research continue to develop, more individual cases may eventually receive convincing explanations. The overall picture emerging from decades of investigation is that The Hum is best understood as a family of related phenomena rather than a single mysterious signal, and that UFO speculation reflects the human tendency to seek extraordinary explanations when ordinary ones remain incomplete.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.international.gc.ca/department-ministere/windsor_hum_results-bourdonnement_windsor_resultats.aspx?lang=eng&amp;pedisable=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: international.gc.ca">[gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">international.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Global Affairs Canada</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Summary of the ‘Windsor Hum Study’ Results...</span></span></span>
